ROBLOX-STYLE 3D VISUAL QUALITY RULES

Purpose

Use the supplied Roblox-style character rigs as the character language, but do not reduce the entire world to primitive blocks. The characters may be stylized and blocky; the environments, vehicles, props, architecture, and other objects must be designed as high-quality 3D assets with convincing detail, scale, materials, lighting, and depth.

Core rule

Do not create blocky placeholder environments or crude block-built objects when the story requires a recognizable real-world asset. Spend the necessary production time on the environment and object quality so the final result feels like a polished 3D animated film rather than a collection of unfinished primitives.

Environment quality

Construct environments as complete, believable 3D spaces. Include real architectural forms, layered foreground/midground/background composition, correct proportions, surface variation, bevels and rounded edges where appropriate, realistic material response, contact shadows, reflections, ambient occlusion, atmospheric depth, and details that support the location and story.

Do not represent an entire street, room, city, garage, school, shop, or landscape with a flat plane and a few cubes. Build the visible portions of the location with enough geometry and depth to support camera movement, parallax, character interaction, and close or medium shots. Add practical details such as doors, windows, trim, signs, lights, furniture, pavement, curbs, plants, cables, vents, shelves, road markings, and surface wear when they belong to the setting.

Vehicle quality

If a car, truck, bus, motorcycle, emergency vehicle, boat, aircraft, or other vehicle appears, create a high-quality recognizable vehicle using code and procedural 3D construction. Do not make a car from a few unmodified boxes.

A coded vehicle should include a coherent body silhouette, hood, roof, cabin, windows, wheel arches, wheels and tires, bumpers, lights, mirrors, doors, handles, grille, panels, seams, and other defining parts appropriate to the vehicle. Use bevelled geometry, curves, cylinders, custom profiles, layered materials, and correctly proportioned components. Wheels must be round and visibly functional. Windows should use a distinct glass material, tires should have rubber material, lights should have lenses or emissive elements, and painted panels should have controlled roughness and highlights.

Vehicles must be scaled consistently with the characters and environment. Their placement, wheel contact, shadows, reflections, doors, lights, and movement must remain continuous between shots. When a vehicle is important to the story, provide close-up inserts or detail shots instead of hiding its deficiencies with distant framing.

Prop quality

Create important props as complete, purpose-built assets rather than generic cubes. A phone, weapon, tool, camera, document, suitcase, machine, sign, piece of furniture, appliance, or instrument should have a recognizable silhouette, appropriate dimensions, functional parts, material separation, and enough detail for its intended camera distance.

Use procedural geometry, layered meshes, curves, bevels, textures, decals, labels, fasteners, controls, seams, handles, hinges, and small secondary details when they improve recognition or storytelling. Props that characters hold or operate must be sized correctly and have clear interaction points.

Code and implementation

Build detailed assets with Three.js or the selected 3D code pipeline using reusable procedural functions and parameters. Prefer custom geometry, extruded profiles, bevelled shapes, lathed or revolved forms, curves, cylinders, planes with proper materials, and modular asset components over a pile of default cubes. Use instancing for repeated details without sacrificing the visual result.

Keep the code maintainable: create reusable constructors for vehicles, buildings, furniture, foliage, signs, and other asset categories. Expose dimensions, colors, materials, detail levels, and variation parameters so assets can be adjusted to the shot. Use a lower detail level only for objects that are genuinely distant and will not be inspected by the camera.

Lighting and rendering

Use lighting that reveals the geometry and materials. Provide a motivated key light, soft fill, readable contact shadows, edge or rim separation when needed, ambient occlusion or an equivalent depth cue, and reflections appropriate to glass, metal, paint, water, and polished surfaces. Do not use flat unlit colors for important assets unless the story specifically calls for that style.

Match the environment lighting to the time of day, weather, and mood. Ensure objects sit on the ground, cast shadows in the correct direction, and receive light from the same world as the characters. Use atmospheric perspective and depth so detailed environments remain readable without competing with the story.

Camera and quality control

Choose camera distances that show the quality of the assets. Do not conceal unfinished work through excessive blur, darkness, fog, extreme distance, or constant rapid cuts. For each important environment or object, include at least one shot where its form and detail are clearly readable.

Before approving a scene, check that:

- The environment is genuinely 3D and supports camera parallax.
- Important cars and objects are not crude block constructions.
- Vehicle silhouettes, wheels, windows, lights, and materials are believable.
- Props have recognizable forms and functional interaction points.
- Scale and proportions are consistent with the characters.
- Materials, shadows, reflections, and lighting match the scene.
- Foreground, midground, and background contain intentional detail.
- No visible placeholder geometry remains in a final shot.
- Detail level is appropriate to the camera distance and story importance.
- The scene is worth the additional production time spent on quality.

Priority

When production time is limited, prioritize quality in this order: assets in close-up, objects directly involved in the action, vehicles and props that identify the story, the immediate environment around the characters, and finally distant background dressing. Do not sacrifice the quality of a key asset merely to add more low-quality objects.
